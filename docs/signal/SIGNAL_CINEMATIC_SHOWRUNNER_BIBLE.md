# SIGNAL Cinematic Story: showrunner bible (Seasons 1–6)

Direction from the founder, 12 Nov 2026, 11:35 CT: *be creative but controlled; the best AV/IT technical learning story mode; realistic
AV environments and ultra-realistic troubleshooting under the pressure of the moment; stay calm and move at your effective pace; move
with the urgency of the situation, not the emotion of the moment; stay professional, speak and respond calmly; escalate properly, with
evidence and facts; document everything — it may not matter to you now, but your future self will thank you for writing it down.*

## What is fixed, and where creativity lives

- **Fixed:** the canon (`packages/signal-canon`, frozen): every chapter, scene, line, decision, option, consequence, weight and lesson.
  Cinematic adds no words to a character's mouth and removes none.
- **Creative (controlled):** how the canon is staged: shot order within an act as the canon gives it, pacing and holds, camera motion on
  the canon's own art (a slow push, never a shake), sound perspective, the voice performance's direction, and the moments the scene
  holds still for the viewer to think.

## The performance standard (every recorded line)

1. **Calm under pressure.** Urgency shows in *pace and precision*, never in panic. Short sentences, clear consonants, no shouting, no
   breathless delivery. A line said in a crisis sounds like a professional on a radio, not a person in a film trailer.
2. **Facts before feelings.** When a character reports a problem, the voice lands on the fact ("ten seats", "row eight", "five
   milliseconds"), not on the drama.
3. **Escalation is a skill, not a failure.** Lines where a character escalates are delivered with confidence: who, what was observed,
   what was tried, what is needed.
4. **Ray** (Paul, Southern, from Alabama): unhurried, dry, warm; his silences are teaching. **Maya** (Sarah, Midwestern): early seasons
   curious and a little quick, settling season by season into the calm authority of the later architect.
5. **Never** comic accents, never mocking a region, never a real person's voice.

## The pacing standard (every scene)

- A decision **holds the scene**; nothing advances until the viewer chooses, and the clock (when on) is the story's, never the
  player's: Classic's "No clock" and "Double time" apply.
- After a costly choice the consequence is shown plainly, without music swelling or punishment theatre; the recovery path is shown.
- Unsafe options (ladders, live power, unapproved access) are presented as the canon presents them: *not yours*, and the scene says so.
- "Document everything": scenes that show a note, a log, a ticket or a photo hold on it long enough to read; the reading time is the
  caption's, and the viewer can pause.

## What the viewer always has

Captions for every line, the written story one click away with the same state, pause / back / next, reduced motion respected, and
nothing timed against them. Media that is missing falls back to captions; never to silence or a broken player.
